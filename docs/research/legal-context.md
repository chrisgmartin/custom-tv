# Legal and regulatory context for Samsung smart TV data collection (ACR), as of 15 October 2026

## 1. Texas Attorney General ACR lawsuits (filed December 2025)

### Takeaway
Texas sued Samsung, Sony, LG, Hisense and TCL in mid-December 2025 under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA). Samsung settled first, on 26 February 2026, agreeing to collect ACR data only after express consent. LG settled on 11 May 2026. The press releases mention no money in either deal. Hisense is under a temporary restraining order (TRO). As of October 2026 I found no settlement or ruling in the Sony, Hisense or TCL cases.

### Cited Findings
- **Filing:** Paxton announced suits against all five makers. Secondary sources date the filing 15 December 2025 (local press covered it on 16 December). — [Texas AG press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-sues-five-major-tv-companies-including-some-ties-ccp-spying-texans); [The Record](https://therecord.media/texas-sues-5-smart-tv-makers-over-acr-tech); [Fox 4](https://www.fox4news.com/news/texas-lawsuit-says-5-tv-companies-spying-content-recognition-tech); [State of Surveillance roundup](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/smart-tv-acr-lawsuits-samsung-settlement-roundup-2026/)
- **Samsung petition, primary source.** "Texas' Original Verified Petition, Application for Temporary and Permanent Injunctions and Request for an Ex Parte Temporary Injunction" against Samsung Electronics America, Inc. and Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd., under Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.46 (DTPA). The caption names Collin County District Court, but the venue paragraph says Comal County, so the petition contradicts itself. — [Samsung petition PDF](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/Samsung%20TV%20Petition%20Filed.pdf)
- **Key allegations in the petition:**
  - ACR captures "audio and video from the more than 73 million Samsung Smart TVs in the U.S. every 500 milliseconds." That includes external inputs such as cable, satellite, HDMI devices and game consoles.
  - The consent screens use the "non-intuitive" name "Viewing Information Services".
  - The notice states that "Samsung neither collects any video footage nor any content displayed on the Device," which the petition calls false.
  - Setup offers a one-click opt-in. Opting out "requires approximately 15+ clicks" across "four or more separate menus", and the user must turn off both Viewing Information Services and Interest-Based Ads. The petition calls this an unlawful dark pattern.
  - Samsung shares viewing data with "demand-side platforms, ad networks, measurement companies, data brokers, and cross-device graphing companies."
  - Source for all of the above: [Samsung petition](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/Samsung%20TV%20Petition%20Filed.pdf)
- **Relief sought:** civil penalties of up to $10,000 per DTPA violation, plus up to $250,000 more where the practice targeted consumers aged 65 or older, and temporary and permanent injunctions. The petition also served notice under the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA, § 541.154). Texas said it would add TDPSA claims if Samsung did not cure within 30 days. — [Samsung petition](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/Samsung%20TV%20Petition%20Filed.pdf)
- **Evidence-gathering:** an OAG employee bought a Samsung 50" U7900 Tizen TV (2025) at Best Buy in Austin on 3 December 2025 and documented the setup screens. — [Samsung petition](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/Samsung%20TV%20Petition%20Filed.pdf)
- **Samsung settlement, 26 February 2026:**
  - Samsung Electronics America "must halt any collection or processing of ACR viewing data without obtaining Texas consumers' express consent."
  - It must "promptly update its smart TVs and implement disclosures and consent screens that are clear and conspicuous."
  - The press release states no money term.
  - It says the cases against Sony, LG, Hisense and TCL "remain ongoing".
  - Source: [Texas AG press release, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-major-agreement-samsung-ensure-texans-are-protected-smart-tvs)
- **Samsung's response:** Samsung said its original privacy policy complied with Texas rules, that its TVs "do not spy on customers", and that users can change privacy settings at any time. — [The Record, 27 Feb 2026](https://therecord.media/samsung-updates-acr-privacy-practices-texas)
- **No fine:** secondary sources say the Samsung deal includes no fine. — [Dallas Express](https://dallasexpress.com/state/privacy-victory-paxton-samsung-deal-explicit-consent-required-for-smart-tv-data/); [secureprivacy.ai](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/samsung-lg-texas-acr-settlements-smart-tv-consent-ctv-publishers)
- **Texas-only scope:** the Samsung relief covers Texas consumers only. — [Malwarebytes, March 2026](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/03/samsung-tvs-stop-spying-on-viewers-in-texas-heres-how-to-disable-acr-anywhere); [Privacy Guides, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2026/03/02/samsung-forced-to-halt-data-collection-in-tvs-in-texas-without-express-consent/)
- **LG settlement, 11 May 2026 (Agreed Final Judgment):**
  - LG will not use ACR to collect viewing data without informed consent.
  - It must show a pop-up disclosure on its TVs and post that disclosure on its website.
  - Users get "a clear and simple way to opt out."
  - LG may not transfer viewing data "in any form to the Chinese Communist Party."
  - The deal "builds upon" the Samsung settlement.
  - "Cases against Sony and Hisense and TCL Technology … remain ongoing."
  - Source: [Texas AG press release, 11 May 2026](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-major-agreement-lg-protect-texans-privacy-and-stop-data-being)
- **Hisense TRO:** Texas obtained a TRO barring Hisense from collecting, using or selling Texas ACR data, which the AG called a first-of-its-kind order. The case is Cause No. 380-09751-2025, which suggests the 380th District Court in Collin County. Secondary sources date the TRO 17 December 2025, but a law-firm post places it in January 2026. — [Texas AG press release on the Hisense TRO](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-court-order-stopping-ccp-aligned-smart-tv-company-spying-texans); [TRO PDF](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/TRO_0.pdf); [State of Surveillance](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/smart-tv-acr-lawsuits-samsung-settlement-roundup-2026/); [Alston & Bird privacy blog](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/texas-court-blocks-smart-tv-data-collection/)
- **Sony and TCL:** coverage describes both cases as quiet, with no reported settlement. — [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/acr-lawsuits-smart-tv-spying-cases-and-settlements/); [secureprivacy.ai](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/samsung-lg-texas-acr-settlements-smart-tv-consent-ctv-publishers)
- **AG transition:** a prediction market notes that a new Texas AG takes office in January 2027, which could affect the cases still open. — [WahooPredict](https://wahoopredict.com/en/events/will-texas-settle-its-smart-tv-acr-spying-lawsuit-with-sony-hisense-or-tcl-by-march-31-2027)

### Inferences
- The Samsung deal is a behaviour-only settlement limited to Texas, built around opt-in consent. US owners outside Texas get no direct legal benefit. Any product change Samsung ships nationally would be voluntary. The Tillery class action (section 2) argues that Californians have not received the same protection.
- The LG terms (pop-up disclosure, simple opt-out, no data transfer to China) appear stricter or at least more specific than the Samsung press release. Samsung's settlement document itself was not available to check.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain the Samsung settlement text. I do not know whether it is an Agreed Final Judgment or an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance, its exact compliance deadlines, or whether it orders deletion of past Texas data. The Tillery complaint says Samsung "accepted a permanent injunction". The AG press release says only "agreement".
- I found no confirmed docket activity after March 2026 for Sony, Hisense or TCL. Whether the Hisense TRO became a temporary injunction is unknown.
- One secondary source reports that Kentucky passed ACR-specific legislation (HB 692), but I did not verify this ([State of Surveillance](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/smart-tv-acr-lawsuits-samsung-settlement-roundup-2026/)).

## 2. FTC precedent, US class actions, and Samsung's earlier controversies

### Takeaway
FTC v. Vizio (February 2017) remains the main federal ACR precedent: Vizio paid $2.2M and agreed to affirmative express consent. I found no later FTC enforcement action aimed at a TV maker's ACR. Samsung faces two pending private class actions, one in New York filed 9 January 2026 and one in California filed 6 October 2026. Both rely partly on the Video Privacy Protection Act (VPPA), and neither has produced a ruling or settlement that I could find. An earlier wiretap case over Samsung TVs, White v. Samsung, came out of the 2015 era.

### Cited Findings
- **FTC v. Vizio:**
  - On 6 February 2017 the FTC and the New Jersey AG settled with Vizio for $2.2M over viewing data from about 11 million TVs.
  - Vizio TVs made since February 2014 captured "second-by-second information about video displayed". Vizio added demographic data and sold the result to third parties for ad targeting.
  - The order required prominent disclosure and affirmative express consent, deletion of data collected before 1 March 2016, a privacy program and biennial assessments.
  - Sources: [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/45163); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/02/06/vizio-settles-for-2-2-million-in-ftc-suit-over-snooping-on-consumers-viewing-habits/)
- **Vizio payment split:** $1.5M went to the FTC and $1M to New Jersey, with $300K of the New Jersey amount suspended, so $2.2M was paid. A search summary called these figures inconsistent, but they add up once the suspended amount is taken out. — [Sound & Vision](https://www.soundandvision.com/node/116416)
- **DiGiacinto v. Samsung Electronics America, Inc.:**
  - S.D.N.Y. No. 1:26-cv-00196, filed 9 January 2026, assigned to Judge Gregory H. Woods.
  - The complaint alleges ACR captures on-screen images and audio about every half-second and shares the data with advertisers without informed consent.
  - The VPPA is the core federal claim. The named plaintiffs come from several states.
  - Docket entries appear on 17 March, 12 May and 29 June 2026, but I could not read their content. The case was not terminated as of the last CourtListener update.
  - Sources: [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72125777/digiacinto-v-samsung-electronics-america-inc/); [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/lawsuit-news/samsung-class-action-alleges-tvs-illegally-track-viewing-data-to-sell-for-profit/); [OpenClassActions](https://openclassactions.com/lawsuits/samsung-smart-tv-acr-viewing-data-privacy-class-action-lawsuit.php)
- **Tillery v. Samsung Electronics America, Inc.:**
  - N.D. Cal. No. 3:26-cv-11448, filed 6 October 2026 by Siri & Glimstad. Plaintiffs: Danielle Tillery, Michael Walsh and David Rosenberg.
  - Claims: Federal Wiretap Act, VPPA, California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA), Consumers Legal Remedies Act (CLRA), Unfair Competition Law (UCL), invasion of privacy, unauthorized computer access, negligence and unjust enrichment.
  - Proposed classes: nationwide classes of Samsung ACR users since 1 January 2013, plus California subclasses.
  - Allegations: screen capture about every 500ms; the 15+ click opt-out; a December 2023 Samsung Ads plan to match viewing data with Experian identity records covering about 128 million households.
  - Relief sought: damages and a public injunction requiring separate, clear consent in California.
  - Status: filed, with no settlement.
  - Sources: [ClaimDepot](https://www.claimdepot.com/cases/samsung-smart-tv-lawsuit-alleges-secret-acr-surveillance-of-73-million-viewers); [CourtListener search result](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74924081/tillery-v-samsung-electronics-america-inc/)
- **Contested VPPA question:** whether a TV maker counts as a "video tape service provider" under the VPPA is unsettled. Defences likely include consent, privacy-policy disclosures and the wiretap party exception. — [Rain Intelligence](https://www.rainintelligence.com/blog/smart-tv-acr-surveillance-vppa-china)
- **White v. Samsung Electronics America (earlier smart TV privacy suit):**
  - Plaintiffs alleged Samsung smart TVs record what consumers watch.
  - The court granted an early motion to dismiss, then let Wiretap Act claims proceed after an amended complaint.
  - The Third Circuit later affirmed that Samsung had waived its right to arbitrate.
  - Source: [Bernstein Liebhard case page (PDF)](https://www.bfalaw.com/cases/white-v-samsung-electronics-america-inc-et-al/_pdfx/white-v-samsung-electronics-america-inc-et-al.pdf)
- **2015 voice recognition controversy, February 2015:**
  - Samsung's Smart TV privacy policy warned that spoken words containing personal information "will be among the data captured and transmitted to a third party."
  - Samsung reworded the policy. Voice commands are collected only when the user presses the activation button and speaks into the remote. They go to a third-party provider, "currently, Nuance Communications, Inc."
  - Samsung said it "does not retain voice data or sell it to third parties."
  - Sources: [Samsung US newsroom statement](https://news.samsung.com/us/?p=290); [CS Monitor](https://www.csmonitor.com/layout/set/amphtml/Technology/2015/0210/Samsung-rewords-Orwellian-privacy-agreement-after-pressure); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2015/02/09/samsung-smart-tv-voice); [Dallas News](https://dallasnews.com/news/2015/02/10/shhh-that-smart-tv-might-be-eavesdropping)

### Inferences
- Vizio set the US standard that ACR needs prominent disclosure and affirmative express consent. The Texas settlements apply the same principle under state consumer-protection law nine years later.
- The private cases are at an early stage. As of October 2026, no court has decided whether ACR data sharing by a TV maker violates the VPPA or the Wiretap Act.

### Gaps
- I found no FTC enforcement action or formal guidance on smart TV ACR after 2017. I could not confirm from FTC.gov whether any staff report or blog post since then has addressed ACR.
- I could not read the DiGiacinto docket entries. It is unknown whether Samsung moved to dismiss or to compel arbitration, or whether the case was transferred or consolidated with Tillery.
- I could not confirm White v. Samsung's case number, filing year (believed to be about 2015 in D.N.J.) or final outcome.

## 3. Consumer rights: state privacy laws, Global Privacy Control, and requests to Samsung

### Takeaway
About 20 US states now have comprehensive privacy laws that give residents rights to access, delete and correct their data and to opt out of targeted ads and "sale". These include California, Texas, Virginia, Colorado and Connecticut. Samsung handles requests through its US Privacy Rights Portal (samsung.com/us/privacy/rights/) or 1-800-SAMSUNG. Samsung honors Global Privacy Control (GPC) only for targeted ads on Samsung.com in that browser. On the TV itself, the opt-out is the TV's settings.

### Cited Findings
- **Number of states:** at least 20 states have comprehensive privacy laws in effect. Indiana, Kentucky and Rhode Island took effect on 1 January 2026, and Arkansas in July 2026. Sources dated August 2026 report Oklahoma and Alabama laws arriving in 2027. Counts differ across trackers. — [compliancehub.wiki](https://compliancehub.wiki/2026-state-privacy-law-patchwork-20-states-cppa-enforcement-escalation/); [Enzuzo tracker](https://www.enzuzo.com/blog/us-state-privacy-laws); [IAPP key dates](https://iapp.org/resources/article/key-dates-from-us-comprehensive-state-privacy-laws/)
- **Universal opt-out signals (GPC):** about 12 states require businesses to honor universal opt-out signals such as GPC, with Connecticut and Oregon adding the mandate in 2026. Named lists include California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, New Jersey, New Hampshire, Oregon and Texas. Sources disagree; one September 2026 list names only 7 states, including Nebraska. — [ClickPoint](https://blog.clickpointsoftware.com/state-opt-out-requirements); [CookieScript](https://cookie-script.com/privacy-laws/universal-opt-out-mechanisms-in-us-privacy-laws-gpc-requirements-by-state/amp); [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/regulations/universal-opt-out-2026/)
- **Samsung's US Privacy Policy and US Supplement (effective 20 July 2026):**
  - Rights: residents of covered states may opt out of sale, of sharing for cross-context behavioral advertising, of targeted advertising, and of sensitive-data processing. They may also request a list of third parties that received their data.
  - Requests and portability: they can request access (including in a structured, machine-readable format), deletion and correction.
  - How to submit: the portal at https://www.samsung.com/us/privacy/rights/ or 1-800-SAMSUNG (1-800-726-7864). The policy covers authorized agents and appeals.
  - Other contacts: the California Consumer Privacy Statement at /us/account/privacy-policy/california/, the webform at privacypost.sea.samsung.com/ccpa/privacy-inquiries, and the Privacy Office at 700 Sylvan Ave, Englewood Cliffs, NJ 07632.
  - Source: [Samsung US Privacy Policy](https://www.samsung.com/us/account/privacy-policy/)
- **Samsung's GPC position:** opting out of targeted ads "on Samsung.com" works by enabling an opt-out preference signal in the browser, set separately for each browser and device. Samsung does not respond to signals that fall short of state-law requirements, such as legacy Do Not Track. — [Samsung US Privacy Policy](https://www.samsung.com/us/account/privacy-policy/)
- **Samsung's ACR support page:** "you have the right to opt out of ACR and the collection of your viewing history at any time" via the TV's Settings menu. — [Samsung ACR support page](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10010616/)
- **Weak access responses:** UCL researchers (2024) filed GDPR access requests with Samsung and LG. They found the responses vague and not matched to the volume of data they saw the TVs send. — [UCL news, Nov 2024](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2024/nov/smart-tv-tracking-raises-privacy-concerns/)
- **EU/UK:**
  - UCL's 2024 study, presented at ACM IMC 2024, tested Samsung and LG TVs in the UK and US. ACR was on by default on many TVs and stopped when users opted out.
  - Italian press reporting of the same research describes Samsung and LG TVs sending screen snapshots about every 15 seconds. That is a different interval from the 500ms Texas alleges.
  - I found no EU or UK regulator enforcement action against Samsung over ACR.
  - Sources: [UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2024/nov/smart-tv-tracking-raises-privacy-concerns/); [money.it](https://www.money.it/la-smart-tv-spia-lg-samsung-condividono-screenshot-del-schermo-ogni-15-secondi)

### Inferences
- A practical sequence for a US owner:
  1. Turn off Viewing Information Services and Interest-Based Ads on the TV. These are the two settings the Texas petition names.
  2. Submit an opt-out of sale, sharing and targeted ads through samsung.com/us/privacy/rights/. The Samsung account tied to the TV is presumably needed so the request maps to TV identifiers; this is unverified.
  3. Optionally request access to, or deletion of, the data held.
  4. If Samsung denies the request, use the appeal route the policy describes. Residents of states with an AG complaint channel can escalate there.
- Residents of states without a comprehensive privacy law get no statutory right to deletion. They still have Samsung's own TV-setting opt-out and the general portal, which Samsung may honor voluntarily.
- GPC does not reach the TV. It is a browser signal, and Samsung applies it only to Samsung.com.
- Under GDPR and the ePrivacy rules, EU and UK users generally must give opt-in consent for this kind of tracking. The UCL result that opt-out stops ACR is consistent with that. This is general background; I found no regulator decision specific to Samsung ACR.

### Gaps
- I could not render the Privacy Rights Portal's form (it is script-driven). The exact request types shown, whether a Samsung account login is required, how identity is checked, and the response time are all unconfirmed. The statutory baseline is about 45 days under most state laws; this is not checked against Samsung's page.
- I did not verify per-state statute details (CCPA/CPRA, TDPSA, Virginia CDPA, Colorado CPA) beyond the tracker summaries.
- I found no source on whether Samsung changed its US-wide TV consent screens after the Texas settlement, as opposed to Texas only.

## 4. What Samsung's notices say it collects via Viewing Information Services, who receives it, and Samsung Ads' scale

### Takeaway
Samsung says that when Viewing Information Services is on, ACR collects viewing history (programs, channels, duration), the device's Personalized Service ID and IP address. It says it "does not record or watch any of the content displayed." The data feeds Samsung Ads' personalized advertising and is shared with ad partners. Texas alleges further sharing with DSPs, measurement firms, data brokers and cross-device graph companies. Scale claims range from more than 30 million ACR-enabled TVs (2018) to 73 million Samsung smart TVs in the US (Texas petition, 2025). Samsung Ads claims to reach 160 million US consumers.

### Cited Findings
- **ACR support page:** with Viewing Information Services enabled, ACR "generate[s] unique signatures" and collects viewing history (programs watched and for how long), the Personalized Service ID and IP address. "Samsung does not record or watch any of the content displayed on your Smart TV." The page points to the in-TV Viewing Information Services Privacy Notice and Smart Hub Privacy Notice for details. — [Samsung ACR support page](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10010616/)
- **Samsung Ads US Privacy Notice (effective 30 June 2023):** the collected data includes device viewing history (channels, websites, games, programs). This happens "only if Viewing Information Services is enabled", in which case Samsung "may use automatic content recognition (ACR) and other technologies." The notice explains that personalized ads draw on viewing history. The URL now redirects to the general US policy. — [Samsung Ads Privacy Notice (via search snippet)](https://www.samsung.com/us/account/privacy-policy/samsungads); [archived copy PDF](https://cybershack.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Samsung-Ads-Privacy-Notice.pdf)
- **Australian notice (effective 1 April 2021):** TV viewing history (networks, channels, websites, programs, time watched) is collected when the user has agreed to both "Interest-Based Advertisement Services" and "Viewing Information Services". — [Samsung AU Ads Privacy](https://www.samsung.com/au/info/ads-privacy)
- **US Privacy Policy (effective 20 July 2026):**
  - Data collected from Samsung Smart TVs includes the Personalized Service ID and Tizen Identifier for Advertising, usage and log data, and voice recordings when voice commands are enabled.
  - Recipients include "ad partners", service providers that run advertising, and ad networks.
  - Samsung concedes that sharing data through automated technologies "may be considered a 'sale'" or "targeted advertising."
  - Source: [Samsung US Privacy Policy](https://www.samsung.com/us/account/privacy-policy/)
- **Texas allegations on sharing and the screen-content claim:** Samsung shares viewing data with DSPs, ad networks, measurement companies, data brokers and cross-device graphing companies. The petition calls Samsung's statement that it does not collect video or screen content "a bald-faced lie." — [Samsung petition](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/Samsung%20TV%20Petition%20Filed.pdf)
- **Scale figures:**
  - **Texas petition (December 2025):**
    - more than 73 million Samsung Smart TVs in the US
    - roughly 6.7 million Texans, more than 1 in 5, live in a home with a Samsung Smart TV (figure derived from household counts)
    - Samsung Ads, founded in 2015, is the "monetization arm of Samsung Electronics" and claims to reach "160 million U.S. consumers."
    - Source: [Samsung petition](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/Samsung%20TV%20Petition%20Filed.pdf)
  - **Samsung Ads one-sheet (2018 data):** "30+ million ACR-enabled Smart TVs in the US." — [Samsung Ads one-sheet PDF](https://image-us.samsung.com/SamsungUS/samsungbusiness/samsung-ads/resources/tv-ad-retargeting/090718/SamsungAds_Resources_OneSheet_TVAdRetargeting.pdf)
  - **Adweek profile:** viewing insights from "over 50MM" US TVs. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/company-details/samsung-ads-2/)
  - **The Register (2020):** a Samsung executive claimed over 60% of the US ACR footprint. — [The Register, 30 Sep 2020](https://www.theregister.com/2020/09/30/samsung_smart_tv_ads/)
  - **ARF Dash study (2024):** Samsung's ad reach was 45.54M US smart-TV households, roughly tied with Prime Video. This measures reach, not a TV count. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/408452/samsung-prime-video-have-highest-smart-tv-ad-reac.html)
  - **Experian match (December 2023):** Samsung Ads announced matching with Experian identity data covering about 128M households. This is cited in the Tillery complaint and is not independently verified here. — [ClaimDepot](https://www.claimdepot.com/cases/samsung-smart-tv-lawsuit-alleges-secret-acr-surveillance-of-73-million-viewers)
- **Capture interval conflict:**
  - Texas and the private plaintiffs allege capture every 500ms, citing an academic article in *Convergence* (July 2025).
  - Samsung denies recording screen content and says ACR creates "signatures".
  - UCL's 2024 measurements are reported as about every 15 seconds.
  - Sources: [Samsung petition](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/Samsung%20TV%20Petition%20Filed.pdf); [Samsung ACR page](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10010616/); [money.it](https://www.money.it/la-smart-tv-spia-lg-samsung-condividono-screenshot-del-schermo-ogni-15-secondi)

### Inferences
- The dispute is mainly about how the data is described, not whether it is collected. Samsung frames ACR as "signatures" and "viewing history". Plaintiffs frame the same process as screen capture. Both sides agree that ACR data linked to device identifiers feeds ad targeting.
- Samsung is one of the largest ACR data holders in the US. The best-sourced numbers are tens of millions of TVs and about 45M households reached (2024). No 2026 figure from Samsung was found.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full current in-TV Viewing Information Services Privacy Notice text or a named list of third-party recipients.
- I found no 2026 Samsung Ads figure for ACR-enabled TVs. The 73M figure comes from the Texas petition and is not Samsung's own.
- The Experian 128M-household partnership comes from a plaintiff-side summary. I did not check it against Samsung's December 2023 announcement.
